# Skill: Scene Writing

General scene-writing conventions for NovelVisualStudio projects.
Applies across all genres unless a genre skill overrides a specific rule.

---

## The Beat rule

Every scene must have a beat — one thing that changes.
If nothing changes by the end of the scene, the scene doesn't belong.
The change can be: information revealed, relationship shifted, decision made, illusion broken.
Small changes are fine. No change is not.

---

## Dialogue mechanics

**One voice per line.** Each speech act belongs to one character.

**Action beats replace adverbs.** Not: *"I don't know," she said nervously.* Instead: *"I don't know." She looked at the door.*

**Subtext over text.** Characters who say exactly what they mean are boring. Characters who say one thing and mean another are the story.

**Silence is dialogue.** A character not answering is an answer. Mark it: `*(no answer)*` or `*(pause)*` — and let the next line carry the weight of it.

**Lines that do two things.** Every line of dialogue should do at least two: advance plot AND reveal character. "What time did you arrive?" is plot. "You always were early to other people's disasters" is plot and character.

---

## Scene structure

```
ENTRY   — establish location, present characters, implicit stakes
TENSION — conflict or revelation begins
TURN    — something unexpected (information, refusal, action)
EXIT    — the scene ends differently from how it started; one thread advances
```

Not every scene needs all four in order. But every scene needs a TURN, or it's a summary.

---

## What a scene is not

- A place to deliver exposition disguised as dialogue ("As you know, Bob…")
- A description of what characters are feeling (show the behaviour, not the feeling)
- A setting piece with no human conflict
- A transition ("they drove to the house" is a scene heading, not a scene)

---

## Pacing signals

**Short lines = fast pace.** In a confrontation, lines get shorter as tension peaks.

**Long speeches = control or performance.** A character who speaks in paragraphs is either in power or performing. A character who stops mid-sentence is losing it.

**White space = weight.** A single-line scene can be devastating. Use it for moments that need room to breathe.

---

## The last line rule

The last line of a scene is what the reader carries into the next.
It should either: raise a question, land a revelation, or make a promise.
Scenes that end with logistics lose momentum. Scenes that end on a human note carry it.
