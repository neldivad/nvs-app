## Overview
Sixty desks arranged in clusters of six. No walls. The architecture of a company that decided transparency meant removing every door.

## Description
Hot in the afternoons. The AC is set for a headcount that no longer exists, so the surviving employees are cold. Nobody mentions this in the same sentence as the restructure, but they notice it.

The kitchen is at the far end — a pilgrimage of fifty feet that everyone takes more often than they need to. It is the only socially acceptable reason to walk past someone's desk and assess how they are holding up.

## Seating
- **In Tern**: perimeter desk, window side. Can see into the boardroom glass from here.
- **Ann Jee Nier**: cluster desk, middle row. Equidistant from everyone.
- **Hugh Arr**: back corner. Has a standing desk he never stands at.
- **See Yi Oh**: does not have a desk on the floor. Her office is behind the boardroom.

## The kitchen
The only neutral territory. Coffee, a fruit bowl that is always running low, a sign about cleaning up after yourself that nobody wrote and nobody removes.

Every significant off-the-record conversation in the story happens here or between here and someone's desk.
